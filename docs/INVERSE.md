# Calibrated inverse imaging

The solver uses known-geometry measurement operators. For the diffuse experiment, the illumination intensity, distance, incidence and detector calibration are known. The spectral reflectance is a three-coefficient Bernstein mixture on the wavelength interval. Emitter recovery uses known detector gain and basis, with three nonnegative coefficients. Extinction recovery uses a calibrated incident beam, homogeneous attenuation and known path lengths. The direct rendered calibration is evaluated as a closed-form single-path model. It has no visibility discontinuity or stochastic gradient.

For a measurement y with known noise scale sigma, the objective is half the sum of squared standardized residuals. The local Jacobian of the linear experiments is the calibrated basis. The extinction derivative is the prediction times negative path length. Parameters are optimized directly in bounded physical units. A damped Gauss-Newton direction is projected to the bounds and accepted only when the objective decreases. The loop caps iterations and checks the scaled projected gradient and objective decrease. The inverse information diagonal gives local standard errors only for identified, adequately fitted experiments. Pivot ratios are diagnostics rather than exact condition numbers.

The product model exposes the scale ambiguity in simultaneous light and reflectance fitting. Changing either factor can preserve the measurements, so the solver reports rank deficiency and null standard errors. Zero path lengths and collinear spectral probes create other rank failures. Alternating measurement bias raises the reduced residual and triggers the model-mismatch rule. The confidence diagnostic assumes independent Gaussian measurement noise and fixed calibration. These are synthetic experiments.
